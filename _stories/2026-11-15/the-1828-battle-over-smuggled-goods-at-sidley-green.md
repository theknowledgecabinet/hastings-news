A new BBC feature looks back at the night in 1828 when a chase for smuggled goods turned into one of East Sussex's most dramatic confrontations in the fields around Sidley, the village that long ago became part of suburban Bexhill.

In the early 19th century, smuggling was rife along the East Sussex coast, with the stretch between Hastings and Eastbourne used to bring in tea, tobacco and spirits to avoid high taxes. Local people supported the smugglers, who provided affordable goods and work in difficult times. Julian Porter, from Bexhill Museum, told the BBC's Secret Sussex programme that "smuggling could pay two weeks wages in one go".

One of the most famous encounters took place on the night of 3 January 1828. A large consignment of contraband had been landed on the beach at Bexhill and was being moved inland when members of the government's Coast Blockade Service spotted the operation and launched a pursuit. As the smugglers tried to get their cargo away from the coast, skirmishes broke out in the surrounding countryside, and when they reached Sidley Green, 10 men were arrested and found guilty, Mr Porter said.

The incident was later dubbed the Battle of Sidley Green. Accounts describe dozens of smugglers and their supporters, some carrying long ash poles known as "bats", facing armed blockade men in a fierce struggle. Today, little evidence of the fight remains on the ground, as modern housing and roads have transformed the landscape.
