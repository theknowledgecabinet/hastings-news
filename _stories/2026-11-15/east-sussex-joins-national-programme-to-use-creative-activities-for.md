East Sussex County Council has been selected to join a national programme that uses creative and cultural activities to improve health and wellbeing.

The Creative Health Leads Programme, run by the National Centre for Creative Health, will part fund a new Creative Health Lead role in the county and open the door to expert guidance and support. The role is backed by a two-year subsidy, with the remaining cost covered by ring-fenced Public Health money, the council said.

The Creative Health Lead will work in partnership with the Culture East Sussex board, a network of public bodies, cultural organisations and individuals hosted by the council, whose chair is Stewart Drew, chief executive of the De La Warr Pavilion in Bexhill.

Cllr Mark Estcourt, the council's lead member for adult social care and health, said getting people involved in artistic and cultural activities can improve health, reduce medication use and GP visits, and tackle loneliness, and that the programme could help reduce inequalities in the county's coastal and most deprived areas.
