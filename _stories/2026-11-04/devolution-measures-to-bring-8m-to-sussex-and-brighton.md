Last week's budget included measures to give devolved local government in England a bigger share of its own tax revenues, the Hastings Online Times reports.

From the next financial year, local councils and mayoral strategic authorities in England will be allocated a larger share of business rates, and from 2028/29 areas with mayors will keep part of local income tax revenues. The government intends local and regional government to retain 100 percent of local business rates income by the end of the decade, with the transfer initially managed in a fiscally neutral way.

The Good Growth Fund will start operations in 2027/28 with an initial pot of £285m. The larger share, £165m, is earmarked for areas with a mayor in 2027, while £120m will be incubator funding for areas transitioning towards a devolution deal.

Sussex and Brighton, which are still in the transitioning phase, will get £8m from the Good Growth Fund. The county's acquisition of mayoral status is still 18 months away, and local government reorganisation has been pushed back by a further year.
