Hastings Borough Council is considering a new application to sell alcohol from a convenience store in the town centre. The premises, located at 5 to 6 Harold Place, is currently occupied by Forever Food. The latest bid, submitted by Subash Yogarajah, seeks permission for off-sales of alcohol from 10am to 11pm, seven days a week. The application also indicates plans to rename the business Harold Express.

This is the third attempt to secure a licence for the address in the past 12 months. Two previous applications from Forever Food owner Hetalben Thakkar of V&K Private Limited were refused by council licensing panels in January and August. According to the Hastings Observer, the council expects to hold a similar hearing for this new application.

The previous refusals followed opposition from Sussex Police, East Sussex County Council’s Public Health Department, and the council’s licensing team. They raised concerns about reintroducing alcohol sales in the town centre Cumulative Impact Zone, an area where new applications are normally refused unless they do not add to existing pressures. Sussex Police noted in a previous submission that antisocial behaviour linked to street drinking had reduced since the store’s previous licence was surrendered in 2025.
