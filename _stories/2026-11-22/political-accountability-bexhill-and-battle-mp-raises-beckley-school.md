The most local item of the week was a Topical Question on Monday about physical education funding at a school in the constituency. Dr Kieran Mullan, the Conservative MP for Bexhill and Battle, said that teachers at Beckley Church of England primary school, as well as parents of pupils, had contacted him about the impact of the Government's reforms to the way physical education is funded in schools on the school's provision, in which there had been a massive drop. He asked the Minister whether she would agree to meet him to discuss what is happening in Beckley and at other schools.

Dr Mullan took part in eight of the ten recorded votes in the Commons and did not vote in two: the Immigration and Asylum Bill at third reading (passed 290 to 94) and the Government amendment on the Rosebank oil field (passed 253 to 0). He voted No on the Visitor Levy (England) Bill at second reading, which passed 296 to 96. In report stage for the Immigration and Asylum Bill he voted Aye on new clauses 54 and 50 and No on new clauses 18 and 62, each of which was defeated. He voted Aye on the Rosebank opposition motion (defeated 90 to 270), Aye on the opposition day motion on UK-EU relations (defeated 103 to 274), and No on draft regulations making consequential amendments to the Employment Rights Act (passed 299 to 159). The record shows him voting against most of his party on no occasion.

Dr Mullan is the Conservative MP for Bexhill and Battle and holds the post of Shadow Minister for Justice.

He made six spoken contributions this week. On Tuesday he asked, with the Member for Bishop Auckland as co-chair of the All-Party Parliamentary Group on deep geothermal, whether the Minister would meet them to discuss how a North Sea wealth fund could be used to provide transition jobs for oil and gas workers. On Wednesday he spoke twice on the Rosebank oil field and twice on UK-EU relations.

Three written question answers were also published: from the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero on deep geothermal, from the Ministry of Defence, which said officials responsible for Reserve Forces and Cadets had not held discussions with Wealden District Council about support for cadet facilities at Crowborough Training Camp, and from the Home Office on improvements to those facilities.
