This week's official record for the MP for Hastings and Rye shows a week of routine votes and written answers, with no item relating to the constituency. Helena Dollimore, the Labour (Co-operative) MP for Hastings and Rye, took part in all ten recorded votes in the Commons.

She voted Aye on the Visitor Levy (England) Bill at second reading, which passed 296 to 96, and Aye on the Immigration and Asylum Bill at third reading, which passed 290 to 94. In report stage for the same bill she voted No on new clauses 18, 50, 54 and 62, each of which was defeated. On Wednesday she voted No on the opposition day motion on UK-EU relations (defeated 103 to 274), Aye on draft regulations making consequential amendments to the Employment Rights Act (passed 299 to 159), and on the Rosebank oil field voted No on the opposition motion (defeated 90 to 270) and Aye on the Government amendment (passed 253 to 0).

Ms Dollimore sits on the Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Committee, the Statutory Instruments (Joint Committee) and the Statutory Instruments (Select Committee). The record lists no ministerial or shadow post for her. She made no spoken contributions, tabled no written questions and signed no early day motions this week, and the record shows her voting against most of her party on no occasion.

Three written question answers were published. The Department for Transport said the expansion of Disabled Persons Railcard eligibility criteria was "expected to make rail travel more affordable for more disabled people, including those with non-visible disabilities". The Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs answered questions on breeding fast-growing meat chickens and on rewilding natural landscapes to protect against extreme weather events.
