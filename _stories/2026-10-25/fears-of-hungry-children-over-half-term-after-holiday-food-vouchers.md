Families across East Sussex have not received automatic food vouchers for the October half term after the county council ended the school holiday scheme, raising fears that vulnerable children could go hungry.

The vouchers, which supported households whose children receive benefits-related free school meals, were stopped by East Sussex County Council after the May half term, following changes to government funding. The previous Household Support Fund was replaced by the Crisis and Resilience Fund, which is intended for households facing immediate hardship rather than blanket holiday vouchers.

Barry Ashley, who runs The Grumpy Cook cafe in Hastings, said he understood the new fund was managed by Citizens Advice 1066 and was aimed at sudden financial shocks, which school holidays do not fall into. His cafe will offer free meals to children during the half term, with no adult purchase required, as it did over the summer.

Penny Downes of Rye Foodbank said in July that she was very concerned about the end of the scheme, and the Food Foundation's latest figures show 39.2 per cent of children in the Hastings and Rye constituency are living in poverty. Liberal Democrat opposition leader Cllr Kathryn Field said she was concerned the most isolated families might not access the support and that children risked going hungry.

A council spokesman said support is available for any family in East Sussex that needs it, and that the way it is delivered has changed in line with government funding changes. The council said it would spend £7.3 million over the next year through the Crisis and Resilience Fund, on services including debt advice, community food schemes, holiday clubs with meals and help with household essentials.
