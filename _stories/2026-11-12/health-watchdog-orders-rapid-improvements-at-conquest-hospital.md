The Care Quality Commission has downgraded the urgent and emergency care service at the Conquest Hospital in St Leonards from 'good' to 'requires improvement' and ordered East Sussex Healthcare NHS Trust to submit an action plan showing what action it is taking to make immediate improvements. The hospital's overall rating has moved from 'outstanding' to 'good'.

Inspectors found the department did not have dedicated pharmacy support, so people did not always receive their regular medicines on time, with one person in the department for nearly 24 hours without critical blood clot and blood pressure medications. A lack of 24-hour cardiology cover meant one person waited overnight for a specialist cardiology review, staff said, and there was regularly insufficient paediatric nursing. A minimum of two registered paediatric nurses per shift should have been in place, but the service often had just one, and on 15 occasions in one month none at all.

Leaders had also failed to tackle risks that had sat on the trust's risk register for more than five years, including poor sepsis management and overcrowding, the CQC said. It added that the Conquest's urgent and emergency care service was trialling an AI-enabled patient tracking system that had cut the time people spent in the emergency department by 21 per cent.

Amanda Williams, CQC deputy director of hospitals in the South East, said the services were operating under sustained pressure, with staff struggling to provide safe and effective care.
