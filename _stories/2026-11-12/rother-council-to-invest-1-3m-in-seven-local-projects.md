Rother District Council has approved £1.3m from its Community Infrastructure Levy (CIL) fund to support seven projects across the district, aimed at improving local facilities, enhancing accessibility and promoting sustainability.

The largest allocation, £519,584, has been awarded to Rother Voluntary Action (RVA) to create a new multi-use community hub at 34 Devonshire Road, Bexhill-on-Sea, which will turn a disused building into a flexible space for activities, events, training and service delivery. Rye Leisure Centre will receive £331,000 for a new community café and social space, refurbishment of the gym and sports hall, roof repairs, swimming pool improvements and fire safety enhancements.

A further £310,000 has been awarded to East Sussex County Council to create a new special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) facility at Glenleigh Park Primary Academy in Bexhill-on-Sea, adapting two existing classrooms to provide specialist provision for up to 12 pupils with autism and other SEND requirements.

Smaller awards include £47,000 for accessibility works at the Flatlands Projects Art Centre in Bexhill, £40,956 for solar panels and battery storage at Bexhill Museum, £35,748 to decarbonise Catsfield Pavilion, and £35,000 for traffic calming and public realm improvements along the A28 through Westfield village.

Cllr Teresa Killeen, chair of the Strategic CIL Allocations Panel, said the investment demonstrates how developer contributions can be used to deliver lasting benefits for communities across Rother.
