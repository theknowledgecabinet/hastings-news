The illuminated metal Christmas tree has returned to Hastings town centre for a second year, and with it the debate has returned.

It stands outside the former Debenhams building at the entrance to Robertson Street, replacing the real tree that has stood there in previous years, with colourful changing light sequences.

The tree was designed and made at the Ponswood estate by local firm Metalworx, which specialises in trussing fabrication, and cost around £45,000, the Hastings Observer reports. It was paid for with money from the £20 million Hastings received under a government Levelling Up plan, a previous government scheme rather than part of the £21.5 million the town has been awarded under the current government's Plan for Neighbourhoods. According to the Observer the money had originally been ring-fenced for a new cherry picker, an idea abandoned because of the ongoing costs of running the machine and paying qualified operators.

Town centre manager John Bownas said one reason for trying something different was that real trees in the past had looked wind-blown and scruffy, often with big gaps in the branches. Locals remain as divided as they were last year, with hundreds of people commenting in local Facebook groups, one describing it as 'a total monstrosity and hunk of junk' while another said: 'I love it!'
