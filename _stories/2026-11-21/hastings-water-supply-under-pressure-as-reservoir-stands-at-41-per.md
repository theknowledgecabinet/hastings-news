Hastings households are being told the area's water supply is running well below normal levels, and that winter rain is now needed to top up reservoirs, rivers and groundwater.

Bewl reservoir, which feeds water to Hastings and its hinterland via the smaller Darwell reservoir, is currently 41 per cent full, compared with a usual November level of around 60 per cent, according to the Hastings Independent Press. Southern Water avoided hosepipe bans this summer even though the Environment Agency declared a severe drought in parts of East Sussex, but Tania Flasck, its director of water operations, said the drought had left local water resources under significant pressure.

Southern Water said it is considering whether to seek permission to increase pumping rates this autumn and winter, when rivers are running high, to help refill the reservoirs more quickly, as a precautionary measure. The company said the area's supply also depends heavily on groundwater, making winter rainfall particularly important ahead of next summer.
