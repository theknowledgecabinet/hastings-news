A Hastings-based charity secretary has talked about the mothers living apart from their children, who still feel judged, she says.

Kerry Message, the secretary of Matchmothers, told the Hastings Independent Press that the charity, founded in 1979, supports hundreds of women facing separation from their children for a wide variety of reasons, including family breakdowns where children are put in care, parental alienation, family court proceedings, fostering or adoption, international child abduction and incarceration.

The charity began with a confidential telephone support service and still runs its Matchline helpline. Volunteers who have themselves been separated from their children give advice and signposting, and some, who know how the courts work, attend family court as McKenzie Friends to support people representing themselves.

Message, who was supported by Matchmothers when she was unable to see her own sons, said the social stigma towards mothers away from their children is much the same as it has always been. "These mums still feel judged. When they see families out together, they feel isolated and alone", she said. The charity runs peer support groups, monthly online meetings and face-to-face gatherings twice a year in London and York.
