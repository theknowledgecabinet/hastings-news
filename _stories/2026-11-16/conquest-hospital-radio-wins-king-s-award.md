Conquest Hospital Radio, the volunteer-run station that has been bringing music and company into hospitals in Hastings and the Rother for more than 50 years, has received a King's Award, the highest recognition given to voluntary organisations in the UK.

The station, formally the Hastings and Rother Hospital Broadcasting Service, began in 1975 when a small group of enthusiasts started recording programmes at home and playing them through a tape player in a cupboard at the former Royal East Sussex Hospital in Hastings. The original service was established in 1976 following the merger of two hospital radio groups, and it moved to the Conquest Hospital in 1992, where it continues to operate. Volunteers present programmes, visit wards to collect music requests and dedications, look after the music library, organise fundraising and do the technical and administrative work that keeps the station on air. Patients can listen free of charge through Hospedia Channel 6 at their bedsides, and the station also broadcasts online.

The station celebrated 50 years of continuous broadcasting in 2025. East Sussex's Lord-Lieutenant, Mr Andrew Blackman, said: "This is wonderful news for East Sussex and a tremendous achievement."
