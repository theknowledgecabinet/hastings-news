Battle holds what is said to be the oldest Guy Fawkes bonfire celebrations in England on Saturday 7 November, with the town's tradition tracing back to 1646. St Mary's Church then allocated two shillings and sixpence for gunpowder treason rejoicing, and the Battel Bonfire Boyes have kept the custom alive ever since.

The day begins with a maroon at six in the morning, and the Oldest Guy in the World is led onto the Abbey Green at ten in the morning by Saxon Warriors and a children's procession. A torch-lit procession, joined by bonfire societies from across the county, winds down the High Street before the bonfire is lit on the green outside Battle Abbey and a firework display is set off in front of the Abbey gates.

Battle's Guy has a pear-wood head carved from a local tree in 1795; the body is burned each year but the head is kept for the following one. Roads around the Abbey, including the High Street and Market Road, close to traffic from six in the evening until midnight, and there is no parking on the procession route. Trains are running to Battle from Hastings in the evening. The Bonfire Boyes say the event is not suitable for young children or those with a nervous disposition, and ask people not to bring their own fireworks.
