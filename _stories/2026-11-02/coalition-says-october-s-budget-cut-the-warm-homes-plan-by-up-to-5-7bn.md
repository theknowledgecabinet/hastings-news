A fuel poverty coalition says the government's October budget failed to protect households on the coast from volatile gas prices, and cut the Warm Homes Plan by up to £5.7 billion.

Energise Sussex Coast, which works across the Rother area, and the End Fuel Poverty Coalition said the headline £100 Household Energy Payment for households in council tax bands A to D 'will barely dent' the predicted rise in bills, which are forecast to grow by around £300 a year over 2027.

The coalition said the budget included some welcome measures, such as extending the zero rate of VAT on domestic electricity to 1 July 2027, but that 'we got more piecemeal support and vague future promises'.

The cut to the Warm Homes Plan amounts to a £1 billion direct reduction to fund military spending, plus a further £4.7 billion of hidden cuts in a reallocation of the so-called 'financial transactions' pot. According to Carbon Brief, that could cut funding for the plan from £15bn to as little as £9bn. The £1bn direct cut was achieved mainly by slashing the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, which subsidises the replacement of gas boilers with heat pumps, described in the article as the 'central technology' for reducing the climate impact of heating.
