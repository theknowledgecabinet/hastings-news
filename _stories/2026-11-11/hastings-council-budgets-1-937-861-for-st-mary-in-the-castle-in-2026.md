Hastings Borough Council has budgeted spending of £1,937,861 on the Grade II* listed seafront building St Mary in the Castle in the 2026/27 financial year, with the majority set to come forward as capital spending.

Cllr Julia Hilton, the council's deputy leader and lead member for regeneration, set out the figures in response to a written question from Claire Carr, a former borough councillor and chairwoman of the St Mary in the Castle Community Trust.

The total is made up of £1,247,000 on repairs to the road/roof of Pelham Crescent and £617,000 on 'pre-marketing works'. To date the council has spent £12,332 and £18,755 in those areas, and a forecast shows the budget is still expected to be spent by the end of the year.

The council had also planned to spend £49,150 on day-to-day revenue costs associated with the building, which includes security. To date it has spent £62,452 and is expecting to spend £81,447 by the end of the financial year. It had also planned for a £24,711 repairs and renewals budget; to date it has spent £32,351 but does not currently expect further expenditure in that area before the end of the year.

Ms Carr raised a supplementary question at the meeting on 9 November. She said it had been four years since the doors closed at St Mary in the Castle and a year since it was put on the heritage at risk register, and asked when a report on the condition survey was due to come to cabinet. Cllr Hilton said she had not received a detailed update from officers and added it was 'time we had an update on the next stages', with some of the work having to be done by the council to enable the building to be 'fit for anybody to take on board'.
