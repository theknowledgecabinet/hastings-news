It is a year since Southern Water admitted responsibility for the millions of plastic beads that washed up on the beaches at Hastings, Camber Sands and at Rye Harbour Nature Reserve, and Hastings MP Helena Dollimore has marked the anniversary of what is thought to be one of the biggest plastic pollution incidents on the South Coast of England.

The beads were released from Southern Water's Eastbourne wastewater treatment plant during Storm Benjamin in October 2025, but the company only admitted responsibility two weeks later, after the MP questioned its chief executive on the matter. The beads were later found in the saltmarsh at Rye Harbour, which the MP and the Sussex Wildlife Trust described as "devastating".

In the days after the incident, local volunteers came to the beach with buckets and sieves to help with the clean-up, which the MP said was "a testament to the community spirit in our area". She also negotiated for the ongoing clean-up to be paid for by Southern Water, along with a compensation programme for Camber, Rye and the surrounding area built around projects chosen by the local community.

The Environment Agency has upgraded the incident to its most serious Category 1 level, its investigation is ongoing, and the classification means Southern Water could face the most serious set of sanctions. The water company is also banned from paying bonuses to its bosses or dividends to shareholders while the Category 1 classification stands.
