One of the Rye area's longest established Christmas tree growers is promising a top-quality harvest for the second year in a row.

While some growers in southern England have reported significant losses from this year's prolonged dry spell and late frosts, the Hole Park Estate in Rolvenden, near Rye, is preparing for another strong season, with very few trees lost and exceptional quality in those being harvested from this week.

The plantations, established in the mid-1960s, are said to have been helped by the High Weald's heavy clay soils, which retained moisture through the drought.

Estate owner Edward Barham, the second generation in the family to manage the Christmas tree plantations, said the location in the Weald had worked in their favour, and that this year's crop was among the very best he could remember. The estate covers around 100 acres, with up to 20,000 young trees planted each year and around 16,000 harvested annually.

The estate's Norway Spruce was named runner-up in its category at the British Christmas Tree Growers Association's 2026 National Awards.
