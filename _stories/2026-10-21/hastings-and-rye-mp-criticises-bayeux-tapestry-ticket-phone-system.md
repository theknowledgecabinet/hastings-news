The MP for Hastings and Rye has criticised the phone line the British Museum set up for local residents to book tickets for the Bayeux tapestry exhibition.

The museum offered people from '1066 Country', the towns with the historic link to the 1066 battle, an early chance to book the latest tranche of tickets from Monday this week, provided they could prove they lived in the area.

But Helena Dollimore, the MP for Hastings and Rye, said constituents were being kept on hold for hours. One person booked after two hours and 38 minutes in the queue, she said, and another was on hold for two and a half hours. She called the system 'glitchy and shambolic' and asked why constituents had to work on a phone line when online booking was clearly possible for others.

A British Museum spokesperson said demand for the exhibition had been 'exceptionally high' and that the offer to residents was on a first-come, first-served basis. All residents remain able to book tickets as part of the general sale on 21 October, and a third tranche of tickets is due to be released in the new year.
