Hastings Borough Council is to cut back the gorse that grows in the Firehills area of Hastings Country Park.

Gorse is a native species whose dense, spiny thickets give cover to rare and declining birds including the Dartford warbler, stonechat, yellowhammer and linnet, as well as supporting a wide variety of insect life, according to the Hastings Observer. It is the bright yellow gorse flowers that give the Firehills their name.

The council's Grounds Maintenance Team is starting the third cut-and-collect operation in an ongoing project to restore the reserve's lowland heathland, the council said. Lowland heathland is a rare habitat recognised as a priority in the UK, and gorse, alongside bracken, can grow rapidly and outcompete other heathland plants.

The council said the area will gradually change from dense gorse scrub to a more varied heathland.
