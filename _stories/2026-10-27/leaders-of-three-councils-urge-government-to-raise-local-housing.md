Councillors in Hastings, Lewes and Eastbourne have asked the government to raise the housing benefit rates that help people pay private rents.

The leaders of the three councils have written to Florence Eshalomi, the minister for homelessness, democracy, communities and faith, calling for an increase in Local Housing Allowance (LHA) across their areas. LHA rates, set by the Department for Work and Pensions based on local rent levels, should be updated each year, but the Hastings rate has not been raised since 2024, and before that since 2020.

Research cited by the councils shows that in Hastings, no two-, three- or four-bedroom properties were available to let at LHA rates, with only two one-bedrooms available, and no shared rooms available at the shared accommodation rate.

Cllr Glenn Haffenden, leader of Hastings Borough Council, said that with so few homes that could be rented at LHA rates, "many residents cannot afford to live here". He added that between 2013 and 2023, house prices in Hastings rose faster than anywhere else in England.

Across Eastbourne, Lewes and Hastings, 525 households presented as homeless and were owed a statutory duty over the last three months, and 1,008 households, including 1,041 children, are living in temporary accommodation.
