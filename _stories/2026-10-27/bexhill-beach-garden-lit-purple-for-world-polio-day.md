Bexhill Beach Garden was illuminated in purple on Sunday 25 October in a local symbol of the global campaign to eradicate polio.

Bad weather prevented the event taking place on World Polio Day itself, so the project, led by former Bexhill Mayor Lynn Langlands, went ahead on Sunday with support from the Rotary Club of Bexhill and the Rotary Club of Senlac St Leonards. Deputy Mayor Cllr Paul Peters came along and helped, and donations were accepted, with the amount collected to be matched 2-1 by the Bill Gates Foundation.

Robin Taylor of Bexhill Rotary said purple has special significance in the Rotary polio campaign: in many countries a child's little finger is marked with purple dye after receiving the oral polio vaccine, making it a symbol of immunisation and hope.

Colin Goldsack of the Senlac St Leonards Rotary Club said that since 1985, when there were over 1,000 polio cases a day in 125 countries, the number of cases is now down by 99.9%.
