On this day in 1967, the last engines were serviced and stabled at the St Leonards West Marina motive power depot. This marked the final end to the site's operational life, following its effective closure in June 1958 due to the transition to diesel traction. The depot, originally established by the LB&SCR in 1846, had served the Hastings area for over a century before the shift in technology rendered its steam facilities obsolete.

The station itself, which opened in 1846 as the first permanent station for the area, had already closed to passengers in July 1967. Its goods yard had ceased operations in 1962. The depot’s decline accelerated after the 1923 grouping, though it retained importance until the late 1950s. As dieselisation progressed, the bulk of the remaining locomotive allocation was transferred to Ashford, Brighton, Tonbridge, and Tunbridge Wells West. Today, the site is completely demolished, with the location now occupied by a TK Maxx warehouse car park and a train washing facility.
