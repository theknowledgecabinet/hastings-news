A storm swept away the town's harbour on this day in 1597. Between 1595 and 1597, a large and expensive harbour, known as the 'pier', was built in front of Hastings Old Town. On 1 November 1597, a storm swept it away.

Attempts to bring a harbour back followed. James Burton, founder of the new town of St Leonards, tried but failed to raise funding for a harbour at the west end of Marina in St Leonards in 1831-34. Several ideas for a harbour at Hastings were considered in 1834-40, but came to nothing. In 1865, Britain's leading pier-builder Eugenius Birch produced new plans for just a pier, sited at White Rock. On 18 December 1869 the first iron pile of Hastings Pier was screwed into the seabed, a contract by Laidlaw and Son of Glasgow worth £23,250.

On 5 August 1872 the new Hastings Pier was officially opened by the Earl Granville, Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports, on a very wet and windy day. Known as the 'Peerless Pier', it was 910 feet long and 45 feet wide in the middle, and was unique as the first pier in Britain to be built with a large entertainments pavilion as part of its design.
