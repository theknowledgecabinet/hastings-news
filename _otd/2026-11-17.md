Rev. Arthur Eden died in office on 17 November 1908 at St Mary's Church, Ticehurst. He was subsequently buried in the churchyard on 21 November, surrounded by many loving parishioners and friends. The sentiments of those gathered were summarised by lines written about him by parishioner William J. Courthope, who was sometime Professor of Poetry at Oxford.

Father Eden’s incumbency from 1851 to 1908 was a significant point in the parish's history, characterised by rebuilding and reshaping for the future. After serving a curacy in Kent, he was instituted on 16 July 1851 and inducted nine days later. For over 57 years, he lived and laboured at Ticehurst, gaining the respect and affection of his parishioners in both their secular and spiritual affairs. He rebuilt the interior of the church from nearly ground level in 1856 due to significant deterioration and neglect by previous vicars, and arranged for a new vicarage to be built shortly after his arrival.

As a devoted pastor, Father Eden was deeply involved in village life, serving in administrative positions with several local organizations. He personally performed 1,826 baptisms, 1,344 funerals, and 453 marriages during his tenure. His legacy remains visible in the church today; the face of St Mark in the west window of the bell tower, which had faded badly, was renewed in 1984 by inserting that of the parish's beloved Father Eden.
