On 25 October 1705, John Pulteney voted for the Court candidate for Speaker in the House of Commons. This decision came shortly after the 1705 English general election, in which he was returned again for Hastings. Pulteney was an English lawyer and Whig politician who sat in the English and British House of Commons from 1695 to 1710.

Pulteney’s connection to Hastings began when he was returned unopposed in 1695 as the nominee of Lord Sydney, then Lord Warden of the Cinque Ports. He retained his seat through subsequent contests and elections, including in 1698, 1701, and 1702. In 1700, he and Peter Gott funded the building of Hastings Town Hall.

His career extended beyond local politics, serving in various government offices such as under-secretary and later on the Board of Trade. He was defeated at the 1710 British general election but retained his place as a Lord of Trade until 1711. He stood again for Hastings at the 1722 British general election, supported by the Duke of Newcastle, but lost by a single vote. Pulteney died on 23 May 1726. He was uncle to William Pulteney, later Earl of Bath.
