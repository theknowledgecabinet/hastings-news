On 27 October 1858, Frederic Charles Urquhart was born at St Leonards-on-Sea, Hastings, East Sussex. His father served as an officer in the Royal Artillery, while his uncle was a colonel in a cavalry regiment of the British Army. Educated at Bloxham School in Oxfordshire and Felsted School in Essex, Urquhart left school at fourteen to pursue a career at sea. He obtained a second-officer certificate in the merchant navy and served as a midshipman in the Royal Navy before migrating to Queensland in 1875.

In Queensland, Urquhart worked in the sugar and cattle industries before becoming a telegraph linesman at Normanton in 1878. In 1882, he was appointed to the paramilitary Native Police, rising quickly to second-class sub-inspector. He was posted to barracks near Georgetown and later to the Carl Creek camp on the Gregory River. While stationed there, he was ordered to remove Aboriginals from Burketown, marching them east to the Leichhardt River. He later transferred to the Cloncurry district in March 1884, replacing an officer killed in conflict with the local Indigenous population.
