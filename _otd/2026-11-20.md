On Tuesday 20 November 1928, five days after the RNLI lifeboat Mary Stanford capsized and the entire crew of 17 was lost, the town of Rye gathered for the funerals of fifteen of the men, buried in a communal grave.

The 39-foot Liverpool-class boat, launched in 1916, had been sent out from Rye Harbour into a south-west gale with heavy rain squalls and heavy seas, to the Latvian vessel Alice of Riga. News came that the Alice's crew had been rescued by another vessel and a recall signal was fired three times, which the lifeboat crew apparently did not see. As she came back into harbour, the Mary Stanford was seen to capsize, and all 17 men perished.

Hundreds of mourners from all over the country attended the funerals, and members of the Latvian Government were among the dignitaries, recognising that the men had died going to the aid of their country's vessel. The crew included three sets of brothers and a coxswain, Herbert Head, buried with two of his sons. Henry Cutting's body was found at Eastbourne three months later and brought home to be interred with his shipmates; John Head's body was never recovered. The boat was broken up at Poplar in January 1929, and a memorial tablet presented by the people of the Isle of Man now stands at Rye Harbour.
