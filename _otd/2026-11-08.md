On this day in 1922, the Pilot Field in Hastings was officially opened with a football match between a Hastings representatives team and Queens Park Rangers. This formal ceremony marked the culmination of a period where the ground had already been in use for two years, serving as a vital hub for local sport since its initial development.

The site, first mentioned in 1560 as 'the Pilate Field', was acquired by the Hastings Corporation in June 1920. The Hastings Sports Association took control in August of that year to prepare the ground for Rock-a-Nore FC. Initially a large slope with a fenced pitch, it was later split into upper and lower levels, with a cycling track constructed around the lower pitch. The ground also hosted cricket and rugby before the official opening.

Today, the Pilot Field remains home to Hastings United men's and women's teams. The club has used the ground since 1985, following a previous tenure between 1920 and 1948. With a current capacity of 4,050, the stadium continues to be a central fixture in East Sussex, having hosted crowds of over 9,000 in the past.
