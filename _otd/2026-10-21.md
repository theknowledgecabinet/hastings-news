On Saturday 21 October 1944, the Hastings and St Leonards Observer printed what the town had not been allowed to know during the war: the full story of the secret plans to evacuate the whole borough in the event of a German invasion.

Complete plans had been prepared for the compulsory evacuation of everyone still in Hastings after the voluntary schemes, with only about a thousand people, Home Guard, civil defence and essential services staff, required to stay behind. The scheme ran in stages: school-age children first, then the remaining civilians, and a joint plan for a grave and immediate emergency.

The Observer described how the Southern Railway worked out a secret timetable of special trains from all five stations between Ore and West Marina, each to carry 800 people, with the entire movement to be finished in three days. Extra luggage had to be packed and left at set points in town for forwarding to reception areas in Kent and Surrey, because evacuees could take only light hand luggage, and a circular went to every mother in September 1941 listing what her child would need to bring.

The plans were kept under revision until late in the war; the Regional Commissioner's direction was only cancelled at the end of September 1944. Hastings was spared the need for them, but the Observer noted the town's civilian preparations would have stood the test and 'a vital factor in the defence of Britain'.
