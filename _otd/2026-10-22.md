On this day in 2011, the first major museum exhibition of Edward Burra’s work in more than 25 years opened at Pallant House Gallery in Chichester, West Sussex. The show ran until 19 February 2012 and was accompanied by a new monograph on the artist by the curator, Simon Martin.

Burra, who died in Hastings in 1976, was an English painter, draughtsman, and printmaker best known for his depictions of the urban underworld, Black culture, and the Harlem scene of the 1930s. His father was a barrister and later Chairman of East Sussex County Council, linking the artist to the region even as his career took him to Paris and New York.

This exhibition marked a significant moment for Burra’s legacy, following record-breaking auction sales in June 2011. It highlighted his status as a highly unusual and gifted British artist, whose surreal and disturbing work deepened as he experienced the tragic events of the twentieth century.
