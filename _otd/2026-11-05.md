On 6 November 1873, the feast day of Saint Leonard, Alexander Beresford Hope MP PC laid the foundation stone for a new building on derelict land adjoining the north end of the old Christ Church. This marked the beginning of construction for a much larger church designed by Sir Arthur Blomfield, which would be built between 1873 and 1875 to supersede the original structure.

The original church opened in 1860 to serve poor worshippers who could not afford pew rents at other local parishes. By 1873, the old building was frequently full, prompting the need for a 1,000-capacity replacement. The new design, described as Blomfield's "finest achievement in Sussex," features a simple Gothic Revival style and continues to serve a large area of St Leonards-on-Sea, including the former parish of the now closed St Mary Magdalene's Church.

Today, the church maintains a strong Anglo-Catholic tradition and is listed at Grade II* by Historic England for its architectural and historical importance. It remains a landmark on one of the town's main roads, with interior fittings considered the best of any church in the borough.
