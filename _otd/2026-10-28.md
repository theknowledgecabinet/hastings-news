On 28 October 1966, Queen Elizabeth and Prince Philip visited the Hastings Embroidery in the Triodome on Hastings Pier, as part of their royal visit to the Hastings area in commemoration of the 1066 battle, the Hastings Observer reports.

The Embroidery was paid for by Hastings Council and made by the Royal School of Needlework in 1965 to commemorate the 900th anniversary of the Battle of Hastings in 1966. It is 243 feet long and consists of 27 panels, each nine feet by three feet, depicting 81 great events. It took 22 embroiderers 10 months to make.

It was displayed in the Triodome in the summer of 1966. The Triodome was a large domed and circular exhibition hall, 105 feet in diameter, made of aluminium, which cost Hastings Council about £12,000. It stood between the two shelters close to the promenade, replacing the moveable bandstand that had been there, which was sent to Warrior Square Gardens. It was officially opened on 25 May 1966 by Princess Alice, president of the Royal School of Needlework.

After the Embroidery was taken down, the Triodome stayed on the pier: the council sold it to the Hastings Pier company in November 1968, and in 1986 it was re-erected at Brighton Pier as the Dome.
