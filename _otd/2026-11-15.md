On 15 November 1865, the carcass of a finback whale some 70 feet long was sold by public auction on the shingle at Pevensey, after it washed up at Norman's Bay two days earlier. A coastguard at the Pevensey Sluice Station spotted the dead animal floating in the water on the evening of Monday 13 November, and it came ashore that night.

The Corporation of Hastings claimed the carcass under the Cinque Ports Charter, but the Crown took possession because it lay below the high-water mark. It was knocked down for £38 to a group of local fishermen and fishmongers, and an estimated 40,000 people came to see it on the beach in the following days.

After its blubber was rendered, the skeleton was reassembled and exhibited at the Central Cricket Ground in Hastings, where visitors paid sixpence admission. It was later bought by the University of Cambridge for £55, and today it hangs in the Whale Hall of the Museum of Zoology, where it remains on public display.
