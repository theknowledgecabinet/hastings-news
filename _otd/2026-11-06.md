On 4 November 1882, lifeboat man Edward Robus was swept away and drowned after the Winchelsea lifeboat, the Storm Sprite, capsized twice during rescue attempts. The vessel had been launched to aid the steamship Medea of Sunderland, which was in distress. While the Rye lifeboat successfully took 16 crew off the Medea, the Winchelsea boat suffered a catastrophic failure in the water.

The Storm Sprite had been a vital asset at the station since 1867. It was a 33-foot boat, funded by a £350 gift from the Solicitors and Proctors Lifeboat Fund and named at the wishes of subscribers. Its loss marked a tragic moment in the history of the Rye Harbour Lifeboat Station, which operated from the west bank of the River Rother estuary. The incident highlighted the severe dangers faced by volunteer crews in the area, a risk that would later claim 17 more lives in 1928 when the Mary Stanford capsized on its return to harbour.
