On 31 October 2006, the last traders on Hastings Pier left the boardwalks. The National Piers Society's news archive records that the businesses on the Promenade Extension, which had remained open, left on that day, and the final event was a Hallowe'en party in the Gritti Palace pub. By the end of it, the structure was padlocked and deserted.

A meeting to discuss the pier's plight was held at the White Rock theatre on 12 November. Mr Harmesh Pooni, speaking on behalf of owners Ravenclaw Investments, said they intended to carry out a full structural survey, which he hoped would be completed by Easter. Repairs to the damaged trusses were estimated at £1.5m, and the Conservative leader of Hastings Council said he would make no promises about compulsory purchase until the results of the survey were known.

The pier had suffered major storm damage in 1990. The closure did not end its story: the structure was 95% destroyed by a fire in 2010, and Hastings Pier Charity oversaw a rebuilding project, with the pier reopening on 27 April 2016, Wikipedia notes. The redeveloped pier won the 2017 Stirling Prize for architecture.
