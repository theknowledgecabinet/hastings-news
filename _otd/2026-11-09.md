This week in November 1883, a trial of electric street lights in the town was switched on.

The lights were switched on on 12 November 1883, after the council gave the go-ahead for a trial in August 1882, according to a Hastings history feature in the Sussex Express.

Before that, a council meeting in January 1881 had thrown out a proposal to examine the cost of using electricity to power the street lighting rather than gas. Electric lights had first come into large-scale use nationally in 1879 and by 1881 were in use in most cities. Many of the councillors were shareholders or directors of the highly profitable Hastings Gas Company, so they threw out the electricity suggestion, the feature reports. The council's agreement with the gas company was to run until 1883.

Then in January 1882 a group of prominent local businessmen and councillors met to discuss setting up a local electric light company. Many of these people were also shareholders in the gas company, and they agreed to hedge their bets by setting up the Hastings and St Leonards Electric Light Company a few months later. They were to use the 'Brush' system of lighting, and they acquired a site for a power station in Earl Street, the building of which is now a carpet and furniture warehouse.

In January 1884 electric lights were installed on Hastings Pier.
