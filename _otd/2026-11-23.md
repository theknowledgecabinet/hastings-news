No 1 Battery at St Leonards was originally designated as the 7th Cinque Ports Artillery Volunteer Corps and was raised on 23 November 1867. The unit formed part of the broader Volunteer Movement that emerged following an invasion scare in 1859. This enthusiasm led to the creation of many part-time units, including coastal artillery corps along the Kent and Sussex coast, which were later brought together to form the 1st Cinque Ports Artillery Volunteers at Dover in September 1862.

By 1 April 1890, the Sussex batteries were separated from the 1st Cinque Ports Artillery to form the 2nd Cinque Ports Artillery Volunteers. No 1 Battery was reorganised as a position battery, working alongside Volunteer infantry brigades with semi-mobile field guns. It was based at St Leonards-on-Sea, serving as the headquarters for the unit alongside other batteries and companies in Hastings, Ninfield, Pevensey, and Bexhill-on-Sea.

In 1899, all artillery volunteers became part of the Royal Garrison Artillery. When the Volunteers were subsumed into the new Territorial Force under the Haldane Reforms of 1908, the 2nd Cinque Ports RGA merged with the 2nd Sussex RGA and transferred to the Royal Field Artillery to form the II Home Counties Brigade in October 1908.
