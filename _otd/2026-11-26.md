This week in 1846, the Hastings line of the South Eastern Railway opened in stages from the north, with the first section commencing on 25 November. The line was later extended from Robertsbridge to Battle on 18 January 1852. It served as a terminus for only a month before the line was completed to Bopeep Junction on 18 February 1852.

The station at Battle was designed by William Tress and listed Grade II in May 1987. Its building features a 13th-century style with tall windows and a porch, influenced by the historic Battle Abbey. Constructed from stone with a tiled roof, it houses a central booking hall and gabled wings. The platforms were originally staggered and did not overlap, a layout that changed before the line's electrification.

Today, Battle station is 55 miles 46 chains from London Charing Cross and is operated by Southeastern. The platforms were extended for eight-carriage trains prior to the completion of third-rail 750V DC electrification in April 1986. All original sidings have been removed to form part of the car park, while the station retains its ticket office and waiting room.
