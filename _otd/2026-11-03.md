This week in 1896, the first moving pictures to be shown in Hastings and St Leonards were seen on St Leonards Pier, the local record shows.

The screening took place on 7 November 1896, making the pier the venue for the town's earliest public moving pictures.

St Leonards Pier had been built as a public amenity, its structure of iron columns and girders similar to Hastings Pier, and it went on to play a part in the social and cultural life of the town.

The pier's pavilion was turned into a fashionable lounge called the Kursaal in 1904.

It was closed and requisitioned by the military at the outbreak of the Second World War, and in 1940 a section of it was removed as a defence measure. It was damaged by a bomb attack that October and hit by severe gales in early 1943.

The final stages of removing the pier's piles were carried out in the summer of 1953, and there is now no surviving remnant of St Leonards Pier.

A trace of the 1896 showing survives today. A small, almost illegible sign on the promenade railings commemorates the film show, alongside an information board near where the pier once stood.
