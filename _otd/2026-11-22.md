On 20 November 1834, a Coastguard boat and her six crew were lost whilst attempting to rescue the crew of the collier Good Intent. This tragedy occurred just four years after the French lugger La Constance was driven ashore at Fairlight in 1830, an event that had previously seen two coastguard officers awarded RNIPLS Gold Medals for their rescue efforts in heavy snow and violent seas.

The loss of the boat and crew in 1834 prompted the establishment of a local fund, which raised £81. This effort resulted in a lifeboat being provided in 1835, built locally by Thwaite and Winter. However, there are no records of any service from this vessel, and by 1851, the boat was deemed to be unfit for purpose. The station would not see a new RNLI establishment until 1858, following another disaster involving the collier Draper.
