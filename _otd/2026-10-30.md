On 31 October 1940, the Battle of Britain officially came to an end. The British recognise the battle's duration as being from 10 July until 31 October 1940, three months and three weeks of fighting that ended in a British victory.

The Germans had rapidly overwhelmed France and the Low Countries in the Battle of France, leaving Britain to face the threat of invasion by sea. On 16 July, Hitler ordered the preparation of Operation Sea Lion as a potential amphibious and airborne assault on Britain. On 1 August, the Luftwaffe was directed to achieve air superiority over the RAF, with the aim of incapacitating RAF Fighter Command; 12 days later, it shifted its attacks to RAF airfields and infrastructure.

It was the first major military campaign fought entirely by air forces. The Luftwaffe proved unable to sustain daylight raids, but its continued night-bombing operations on Britain became known as the Blitz, which lasted until 11 May 1941. Germany's failure to destroy Britain's air defences and force it out of the conflict was the first major German defeat in the Second World War.
