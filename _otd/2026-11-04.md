On this day in 1834, the Duchess of Kent and the future Queen Victoria arrived in St Leonards-on-Sea to much public acclaim. The reception featured a triumphal arch, a 21-gun salute, public addresses, banquets, and other festivities. They stayed at Crown House, the first building completed in the planned resort, throughout the winter until January 1835.

The visit was orchestrated by Decimus Burton, who believed a royal visit would give the town the push it needed to thrive. The visit was an outstanding success, and such royal patronage assured the success of his new town as a fashionable watering place on the coast. Following the visit, the building was renamed Victoria House, though it reverted to Crown House after Victoria became Queen in 1837.

Today, Crown House stands as a Grade II* listed building on the seafront. It remains a significant part of the St Leonards West Conservation Area, representing the architectural and historical importance of the 19th-century planned seaside resort.
