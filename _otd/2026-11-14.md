On this day in 1948, Charles Kenward, an English first-class cricketer, died at Rye, Sussex. Born at Icklesham Manor House in the village of Icklesham, he was the son of Trayton Kenward, a farmer of over 1,000 acres, and his wife Emily Turtle.

Kenward’s sporting career was brief. In 1905, he made a single appearance in first-class cricket for the Gentlemen of England against Oxford University at Oxford. Batting twice during the match, he was dismissed for 43 runs by Robin Udal in the first-innings, while in the second-innings, he was dismissed by Francis Henley for 4 runs. He was not alone in the sport, as his brother, Richard Kenward, was also a first-class cricketer.

Beyond the cricket field, Kenward served as a Land Tax Commissioner for the county of Sussex in 1938.
